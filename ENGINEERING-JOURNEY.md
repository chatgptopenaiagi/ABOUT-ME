# Engineering Journey

[About me](README.md) · [Document index](INDEX.md) · [My projects](MY-PROJECTS.md)

My work on GENESIS follows a growing interest in how separate software tools can become a more coherent engineering environment. I establish the objectives and priorities; Codex assists with the technical work and supplies results for evaluation.

This is a chronology of **documented engineering milestones**, not a complete personal biography. The dates below are confirmed report or verification dates. They do not establish when I first developed an idea or began exploring technology.

## 26 October 2026 — From individual tasks to a controller and laboratory

The recorded GENESIS-1 work brought research, simulation, public data, journaling and controlled trading operations into a common controller. The controller made a useful distinction explicit: an intention, a submitted operation and a confirmed result are different things.

GENESIS-2 then broadened the environment through ten independently maintained open-source trading projects. Their acquisition, dependencies, builds, launches and representative functional checks were recorded separately. The laboratory preserved upstream authorship and local limitations rather than treating a downloaded repository as a completed integration.

**Lesson:** interoperability requires deliberate boundaries, compatible environments and evidence about what actually ran.

## 27 October 2026 — Making the machine's capabilities visible

The GENESIS-3 report documented an expanded Windows software capability registry while preserving earlier records. Its purpose reflected a simple priority: discover and evaluate existing tools before installing replacements.

The initial GENESIS-3.1 report added relationships, dependency checks, workflow history and explanations for tool selection. A successful test could now inform a later session, provided its scope, environment and verification date remained visible.

**Lesson:** stored engineering knowledge is most useful when it distinguishes current proof, historical success and unresolved assumptions.

## 27 October 2026 — Integrating simulated financial operations

GENESIS-4 applied this approach to a shared financial interface. Recorded acceptance covered simulated accounts and payments, balanced accounting, recovery, reporting, API access and agent interaction. A synthetic EUR 100 deposit followed by a EUR 25 purchase reconciled to EUR 75; repeating the transaction identifier did not post it twice.

**Lesson:** a useful automated system must account for retries, failures and interruptions as carefully as successful operations. A convincing demonstration needs observable acceptance criteria.

## 27 October 2026 — Extending reusable engineering experience

The advanced GENESIS-3.1 report followed later that day. It extended capability composition, dependency-risk reasoning, workflow reuse and causal memory for observed failures and repairs. The stage numbers describe related initiatives; development was not a strictly one-way sequence.

**Lesson:** diagnosing an error should leave a reusable explanation and prevention rule. A proposed multi-tool workflow still needs evidence for its particular combination.

## What this progression means to me

My direction has moved from accomplishing specific software tasks toward preserving the knowledge needed to connect them. Clear objectives give an agent something concrete to build and verify. Retained results help future work avoid unnecessary rediscovery, while honest limitations keep ambition connected to evidence.

Reducing repeated technical effort remains an aspiration, not a quantified productivity claim. I want more human time available for exploration, invention and judgment, as discussed in [My Vision](MY-VISION.md).

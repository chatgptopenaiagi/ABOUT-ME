# Personal Statement

I believe that the future of software engineering will increasingly involve humans defining intentions while intelligent systems coordinate the technical work needed to achieve them.

My interest is not limited to individual programming languages or applications. I am interested in how software tools can cooperate, how engineering experience can be preserved, and how artificial intelligence can help people develop increasingly capable technological systems.

GENESIS is how I explore that direction. I define objectives, propose conceptual architectures, choose priorities and evaluate results. OpenAI Codex assists with discovery, implementation, testing and repair, drawing on software created by many other people. I want the documentation to make those contributions clear.

I value evidence because an idea and a working implementation are different achievements. A successful test should say what was checked and under which conditions. A failure should leave behind something useful: an explanation, a verified repair or a limitation that the next session can recognize.

I also want technology to give people more room to think. Reducing repetitive technical work can create space for questions, invention and informed decisions. That possibility motivates me; I do not treat it as a result already proven in every situation.

My aim is to keep developing this approach with clear human direction, honest technical attribution and a willingness to revise ideas when the evidence calls for it.

**Mihai-Bogdan Simion**
